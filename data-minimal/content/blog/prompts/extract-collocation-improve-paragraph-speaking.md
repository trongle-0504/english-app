### Upgrading collocations and verb patterns

This is a core job of the revision, not an optional extra.

While rewriting, actively look for places where the user used flat, generic, or "translated-from-Vietnamese" wording and replace it with a stronger native collocation or verb pattern:

- plain verb + noun → natural collocation
  *have a big effect* → **have a profound impact on**
- vague verb → precise verb pattern
  *make me feel relaxed* → **help me unwind**
- simple structure → reusable spoken pattern
  *it is important* → **it makes all the difference**
- add a natural verb pattern where the sentence invites one
  **end up + V-ing**, **struggle to + V**, **get into the habit of + V-ing**, **there's no point in + V-ing**

Rules for upgrading:
- the replacement must sound **spoken**, not academic — no showpiece words a real speaker would never say out loud
- keep the user's meaning and voice; upgrade the wording, not the idea
- aim for a few well-placed upgrades, not a rewrite stuffed with idioms
- if the user's original phrase is already natural and idiomatic, **leave it alone** — do not swap good English for different English
- every upgrade must be **bolded** so the user can see exactly what was added

### Sentence splitting

Spoken English runs in short breath-length units, not written clauses. While rewriting, break up any sentence that is too long or too dense to say comfortably aloud.

Split a sentence when it:
- runs beyond roughly **20–25 words**
- stacks three or more clauses (*which... that... because...*)
- chains multiple ideas with *and / but / so* without a pause
- cannot be read aloud in one breath at natural speaking speed

How to split:
- cut at the natural pause and start a new sentence
- add a short linker where needed (*And..., But..., So..., That's because..., I mean..., The thing is...*)
- keep the user's exact ideas, order, and vocabulary — only the punctuation and the linker change
- **bold the new sentence boundary and any linker you add**
- do not over-split: a mix of short and medium sentences sounds more natural than all-short sentences

If a sentence is long but flows easily when spoken aloud, leave it alone. Length alone is not a fault.
