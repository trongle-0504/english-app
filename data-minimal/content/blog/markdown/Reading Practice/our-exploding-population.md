# Annotated Passage

A.

Almost everyone thinks about the future. We try to imagine life in thirty, forty, or fifty years. What will life on the earth be like? According to many people, it will not be very pleasant. These people are very worried about the future. One of the main problems which worries them is <T v="tình trạng quá tải dân số" p="/ˌəʊvəˌpɒpjuˈleɪʃən/" t="noun">overpopulation</T>. In 1975 there were about four billion people in the world, and the population was <T v="sự gia tăng dân số" p="/ˈɪnkriːs ɪn ˌpɒpjuˈleɪʃən/" t="collocation">increasing by</T> about 2% every year. At the end of the twentieth century, the <T v="dân số thế giới" p="/ˌpɒpjuˈleɪʃən əv ðə wɜːld/" t="collocation">population of the world</T> reached six billion people.

B.

Why is this <T v="sự gia tăng dân số" p="/ˈɪnkriːs ɪn ˌpɒpjuˈleɪʃən/" t="collocation">increase in population</T> a problem? First, a large part of the growth is taking place in countries that do not always have enough food for their present population. Without a great deal of money, food, and <T v="hỗ trợ y tế" p="/ˈmedɪkl əˈsɪstəns/" t="collocation">medical assistance</T> from other nations, these countries will not be able to <T v="nuôi sống dân số đang tăng" p="/səˈpɔːt ə ˈɡrəʊɪŋ ˌpɒpjuˈleɪʃən/" t="collocation">support their growing population</T>. Many of their people, perhaps millions of them, will die of <T v="nạn đói" p="/stɑːˈveɪʃən/" t="noun">starvation</T> or disease. But overpopulation is not only a problem for <T v="các nước đang phát triển" p="/dɪˈveləpɪŋ ˈkʌntriz/" t="collocation">developing countries</T>. It is also a serious problem for the countries of the world which do not usually have food problems. In <T v="các quốc gia công nghiệp giàu có" p="/ˈwelθi ɪnˈdʌstriəl ˈneɪʃənz/" t="collocation">wealthy, industrial nations</T>, large increases in the population will have <T v="tác động tiêu cực lên" p="/ˈneɡətɪv ɪˈfekts ɒn/" t="lexical pattern">negative effects on</T> life. We will have to heat and cool more houses, apartment buildings, schools and hospitals. We will have to <T v="vận chuyển con người" p="/trænˈspɔːt ˈpiːpl/" t="collocation">transport more people</T> to and from work. For all this, we will have to use more of the world's <T v="tài nguyên thiên nhiên" p="/ˈnætʃrəl rɪˈzɔːsɪz/" t="collocation">natural resources</T>. Perhaps the world will not have enough oil, water, coal, and wood for all our needs.

C.

One solution which many people suggest is to <T v="hạn chế số lượng con cái" p="/ˈlɪmɪt ðə ˈnʌmbər əv ˈtʃɪldrən/" t="collocation">limit the number of children</T> who are born. There are now a number of safe, modern methods of <T v="kiểm soát sinh sản" p="/ˈbɜːθ kənˌtrəʊl/" t="collocation">birth control</T>. A married couple who use birth control can <T v="lựa chọn quy mô gia đình" p="/tʃuːz ðə saɪz əv ə ˈfæməli/" t="lexical pattern">choose the size of their family</T>. A number of countries with large populations have <T v="các chương trình của chính phủ" p="/ˈɡʌvənmənt ˈprəʊɡræmz/" t="collocation">government programmes</T> for birth control. India, for example, advertises birth control on the radio and television. Teams of workers go out into the countryside and teach the people about the <T v="lợi ích của kế hoạch hóa gia đình" p="/ˈbenɪfɪts əv ˈfæməli ˈplænɪŋ/" t="collocation">benefits of family planning</T>. In China, couples who only have one child or no child get apartments before couples with three children or more. In many other countries, birth control is a subject that school students study in <T v="lớp học chuyên biệt" p="/ˈspeʃl ˈklɑːsɪz/" t="collocation">special classes</T>.

D.

But how successful are these <T v="chương trình kiểm soát sinh sản" p="/ˈbɜːθ kəntrəʊl ˈprəʊɡræmz/" t="collocation">birth control programmes</T>? Some are very successful. In countries like Sweden, Germany, Denmark, Switzerland and Great Britain, families are quite small, and the population is increasing very slowly; in some of these countries, it is not growing at all. But these are <T v="các nước phát triển giàu có" p="/ˈwelθi dɪˈveləpt ˈkʌntriz/" t="collocation">wealthy, developed countries</T>, which can feed their people without difficulty. In developing countries, many of the birth control programmes are not successful.

E.

There are a number of <T v="những lý do có thể cho" p="/ˈpɒsəbl ˈriːzənz fə/" t="lexical pattern">possible reasons for</T> the failure of birth control programmes in developing countries. One reason is religion. Many people belong to religions which do not accept <T v="các phương pháp tránh thai hiện đại" p="/ˈmɒdn ˈmeθədz əv ˈbɜːθ kəntrəʊl/" t="collocation">modern methods of birth control</T>. For them, birth control is wrong, and they naturally refuse to listen to <T v="nhân viên kế hoạch hóa gia đình" p="/ˈfæməli ˈplænɪŋ ˈwɜːkəz/" t="collocation">family planning workers</T>. Another reason for the failure of birth control programmes is <T v="yếu tố kinh tế; kinh tế học" p="/ˌiːkəˈnɒmɪks/" t="noun">economics</T>. Often a large number of children are necessary for very poor families in developing countries. Children can work and help <T v="chu cấp cho gia đình" p="/səˈpɔːt ðə ˈfæməli/" t="collocation">support the family</T>. Also, without a <T v="hệ thống phúc lợi" p="/ˈwelfeə ˈsɪstəm/" t="collocation">welfare system</T>, people have to <T v="phụ thuộc vào" p="/dɪˈpend ɒn/" t="lexical pattern">depend on</T> their children for food, clothing, and shelter in their old age. In these countries, people just cannot see any reason for small families.

F.

For other people, birth control is definitely not the solution the world needs for the problem of overpopulation. In their opinion, government should not decide <T v="quy mô gia đình" p="/ˈfæməli saɪz/" t="collocation">family size</T>. It is not right. The solution which these people suggest is to <T v="tăng sản lượng lương thực thế giới" p="/ɪnˈkriːs wɜːld fuːd prəˈdʌkʃən/" t="collocation">increase world food production</T>. They draw our attention to the <T v="tiến bộ mà các nhà khoa học nông nghiệp đạt được" p="/ˈprəʊɡres ˌæɡrɪˈkʌltʃərəl ˈsaɪəntɪsts ɑː ˈmeɪkɪŋ/" t="collocation">progress that agricultural scientists are making</T>. With <T v="sự tưới tiêu" p="/ˌɪrɪˈɡeɪʃən/" t="noun">irrigation</T>, for example, we can now produce food in many parts of the world which did not produce anything fifteen or twenty years ago. We can even grow plants in water without soil. Scientists are also doing <T v="nghiên cứu về" p="/rɪˈsɜːtʃ ˈɪntuː/" t="lexical pattern">research into</T> plants which grow quickly and into <T v="kỹ thuật canh tác mới" p="/njuː ˈfɑːmɪŋ tekˈniːks/" t="collocation">new farming techniques</T>. The governments of rich countries will have to help the poor countries. Without <T v="hỗ trợ kinh tế" p="/ˌiːkəˈnɒmɪk əˈsɪstəns/" t="collocation">economic assistance</T> from the wealthy nations, poor countries will not be able to use the <T v="sản phẩm và kỹ thuật nông nghiệp mới" p="/njuː ˈprɒdʌkts ənd ˈfɑːmɪŋ tekˈniːks/" t="collocation">new products and farming techniques</T> that scientists are developing.

G.

Birth control and an <T v="sự gia tăng sản lượng thực phẩm" p="/ˈɪnkriːs ɪn fuːd prəˈdʌkʃən/" t="collocation">increase in food production</T> are two possible solutions to the problem of overpopulation. Perhaps there are other solutions. Almost certainly there is no one easy answer to the problem: we will have to use a number of solutions. Then perhaps we will be able to solve the problem of the <T v="bùng nổ dân số" p="/ˌpɒpjuˈleɪʃən ɪkˈspləʊʒən/" t="collocation">population explosion</T>.
