# [IELTS 6.0 - Sample WT2 - Agree or Disagree - Đề 2] - Environment

## 🚀 Đề bài

> [People think that the government should increase the cost of fuel for cars and other vehicles to solve environmental problems. To what extent do you agree or disagree?]

---

## 🧠 Dàn ý

<div class="my-6 bg-slate-50 dark:bg-slate-900/40 border border-slate-200 dark:border-slate-800 rounded-2xl p-6">

### **Body paragraph 1:**

#### **Disagree 1: Driving is a necessity for many people**

* **Not a matter of personal choice**
* Live in rural areas / limited public transport >>> have little alternative but to rely on private vehicles (commute to work / school).
* **Financial pressure & no alternative**
* High fuel prices >>> place financial pressure on households without changing habits.
* Ex: Factory worker living 30 km from city with 2 buses/day >>> no choice but to drive >>> punishes ordinary citizens instead of reducing car numbers.

### **Body paragraph 2:**

#### **Disagree 2: Vehicles are not the primary source of environmental pollution**

* **Other major contributors**
* Factories, coal power plants & deforestation produce far more harmful gases and damage environment.
* **Higher fuel prices fail to address root causes**
* Does nothing about industrial pollution >>> air continues to deteriorate.
* Ex: Industrial cities still face heavy air pollution from factories >>> government should tighten regulations on industrial emissions rather than imposing higher fuel costs on motorists.

</div>

**It is sometimes believed that** higher *fuel prices* for cars and other vehicles <u>should be introduced</u> to *address environmental problems*. **This essay totally disagrees with** *this suggestion* for several reasons.

**The first argument given to support my opinion is that** driving is a *necessity* **rather than** *a matter of personal choice* for a large *number of the population*. Many people live in *rural areas* or places <u>where</u> *public transport* is *limited*, <u>meaning that</u> they *have little alternative but to* *rely on* *private vehicles* to *commute to work* or take their children to school. **Therefore**, *a rise in* *fuel prices* <u>would</u> *place considerable financial pressure on* these households, **potentially** <u>making it more difficult for them to</u> *meet their expenses* without significantly *changing their habits*. **For instance**, a factory worker <u>living</u> 30 kilometers away from the city in an area with only two buses a day <u>would</u> *have no choice but to* drive to work. **This illustrates that** increasing fuel prices <u>could</u> punish ordinary citizens **instead of** *reducing the number of cars* on the road.

**Another point behind my belief is that** *private vehicles* are only one of several *major contributors to* *environmental pollution*. Factories and coal power plants produce far more *harmful gases* than cars, and *cutting down forests* also *damages the environment*. Raising the price of fuel does nothing about these *bigger causes*, **and thus** air continues to get worse. In many *industrial cities*, **for instance**, *air pollution* is still serious **because** factories *continue to operate* and *emit large amounts of* pollutants. **This suggests that** governments <u>should</u> *tighten environmental regulations on* *industrial activities* and target the *primary sources of emissions*, **rather than** simply *imposing higher fuel costs on* motorists.

**In conclusion**, **I totally disagree that** increasing fuel prices is an effective way to *tackle environmental problems*, <u>given</u> **the aforementioned arguments**.
